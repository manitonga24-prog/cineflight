Beginner's cheat sheet

The on-screen icons and your first shots, explained simply.

Welcome. This page is meant to be read just before your first flight, or kept at hand during it. It answers two questions: "what does this button do?" and "what do I want to film, and how?". Take the time to go through it calmly at home — you'll fly with a clear mind afterwards.

1. Your two safety reflexes

Like the brake pedal and the steering wheel of a car: even if you master nothing else, these two controls get you out of any situation. Learn their location by heart, at the top of the screen.
ButtonWhat it does, and when to use it
STOP
(red)		Emergency stop. One press cuts automatic following and freezes the drone in place, in a hover. The moment a situation worries you, surprises you or gets away from you, hit this button without thinking. The drone stops dead and awaits your orders.
RTH
(yellow)		Return To Home. One press and the drone interrupts what it was doing to fly back and land at its take-off point by itself. Use it in case of doubt, if you lose sight of the drone, if the battery is getting low, or simply to call it back to you.

2. The on-screen icons, family by family

The flight screen can look crowded the first time. Don't worry: the buttons are grouped into families, and you'll never need to use them all at once.

Flight — where the drone is

ButtonRole
TAKE OFFMakes the drone take off; it rises and stabilises in a hover. This is your very first action, once the drone is connected and the GPS is reliable (green indicator).
LANDLands the drone vertically, right where it is. Use it to set it down at the end, with clear ground beneath it.
RTHAutomatic return to the take-off point, then landing. Your safety net in case of doubt.
STOPEmergency stop: cuts following and freezes the drone in place.

Following — the heart of the app

Following is what makes the drone take care of you all by itself. The button is a two-position switch:

Displayed stateWhat it means
● TARGET
(orange)		The drone is following you automatically right now. Press to stop following and go back to manual.
○ MANUAL
(gray)		The drone follows no one, it is at rest. Press to turn following on — or touch a person on the screen, or show the Presentation tag (0).
Remember: you can always touch a subject on the screen to designate it as the target, whatever the setting. Your finger is universal: it works on anything the drone can detect.

Framing — your size in the image

Like a photographer's zoom: from a tight close-up to a small silhouette in a wide landscape. It is an artistic choice — a close-up creates emotion, a wide shot tells of a place.
ButtonResulting framing
GPClose-up — the face and shoulders fill the image. Intimate, expressive.
AMMedium shot — from mid-thigh to head. A balance between you and a little scenery.
PDFull shot — the whole person, head to toe. Shows posture and movement.
ENSWide shot — you and all the surrounding scenery. Tells the place, gives scale.

Movements — how the drone moves

The movement turns a plain fixed capture into a living shot. Each one tells a different story.

IconMovement and effect
▣Static — the drone stays fixed and just keeps you framed. Plain, stable, ideal to start with.
◎Orbit — the drone circles around you, like a satellite. Spectacular, reveals the scenery from every angle.
↔Tracking (travelling) — the drone accompanies you, moving sideways. A sense of movement and travel.
↑FApproach — the drone slowly moves closer to you. Creates tension, growing intimacy.
⤢Reveal — the drone pulls back and rises at the same time, unveiling the scenery around you. The classic end-of-film effect.
◉Spotlight — the drone stays put, you fly it with the controller, and the camera keeps you framed automatically.
The Spotlight mode (◉) works the opposite way to the others: everywhere else the drone moves on its own while you focus on your activity; in Spotlight, you do the flying, and it just aims its camera to keep you in the picture, like a theatre spotlight.

Camera — what you record

ButtonRole

RTSPStreams the live video over the network, to show it on another screen.

3. What do you want to film? (for new pilots)

A good shot is simply a framing + a movement. You choose how close the drone frames you (section 2), then how it moves. That's all. Start simple: a PD framing (full shot) with a Static (▣) or Orbit (◎) movement already gives beautiful images, with no risk.

First: tell the drone who to film

1Stand in front of the drone, clearly visible.
2Designate yourself as the subject: touch yourself on the screen, or turn on following, or show the Presentation tag (0). The screen switches to ● TARGET.
3Choose a framing, then a movement. The drone handles the rest — you can put the phone away and live the moment.

Your first shots, easy and pretty

You wantSimple recipe
A portrait of me in the sceneryFraming PD or ENS + Static (▣) movement. The drone stays fixed and keeps you framed. The safest way to start.
A shot that popsFraming AM + Orbit (◎). The drone circles you and reveals the scenery. Instant cinematic effect.
Filming me as I walkFraming PD + Tracking (↔). The drone accompanies you sideways. Perfect on a walk.
A nice video endingFraming GP to start + Reveal (⤢). The drone pulls back and rises to unveil the whole landscape behind you.
The tip that changes everything: begin with the Static movement on your first flights. You get familiar with framing and following without the drone moving. Add the orbit, then tracking, once you feel comfortable. And the "Safe values" button in Settings restores a cautious, proven configuration at any time.
Before taking off, the three golden rules: never fly the drone over people (it passes to the side, never over their heads); keep the drone in sight at all times; and wait for the GPS to be reliable (green) before any automatic flight. The "Safety guidelines" page covers everything else — read it before your first flight.
