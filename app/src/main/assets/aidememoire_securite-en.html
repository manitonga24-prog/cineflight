Safety guidelines

Read before every flight. These rules protect others, protect you, and protect your gear.

The golden rule, above all others: NEVER fly the drone over people. The drone passes beside people, never over their heads — not over you, not over spectators. This is not just good practice: it is a fundamental safety rule and a legal obligation. If something fails, a falling drone must never be able to injure anyone.
The right reflex: think of the drone like a car overtaking another. It passes to the side, at a distance, never driving over people. The drone must always stay "in the next lane over".

1. The pre-flight checklist

  • Reliable GPS (green). Wait in the open for the indicator to turn green before any flight, especially automatic. Weak GPS = the drone stays frozen or refuses auto mode.
  • Battery with a real margin. Never start with just enough: plan for the flight, the return, and a calm landing.
  • Clear and legal area. No public, no passers-by where the drone will operate. Check that flying is allowed there.
  • Return To Home (RTH) ready. Know where the button is and that the take-off point is set.
  • A finger ready on the emergency stop. Know the STOP button by heart, tag 10, and the "CineFlight stop" command.
  • Drone in sight. You must be able to see it with the naked eye throughout the flight.

2. People: distance, and never overhead

  • Never over people, nor over the filmed subject, nor over spectators.
  • Keep a safe horizontal distance from anyone not involved in the shoot.
  • The subject stays to the side: when the drone follows or approaches you, it frames you from the front or the side, never passing over your head.
  • Clear space required for automatic following and macros: make sure there is no public or passers-by in the operating area.
  • Orbit and approach at a safe height: set them to an altitude where, in a fall, the drone would not hit anyone.
The approach has a minimum stop distance of 2 metres: the drone will never dive at you, whatever your configuration.

3. During the flight

  • Keep the drone in sight at all times — a legal obligation (in Canada, visual line-of-sight).
  • Doubt reflex: at the slightest problem, bring the drone back (RTH), land, and calmly resume on the ground.
  • Surprise reflex: if a situation gets away from you, hit STOP without hesitation — the drone freezes in place.
  • Respect the legal ceiling (120 m in Canada) and local regulations.
  • Wind and weather: in strong wind, following and voice are less reliable. When in doubt, postpone.

4. Automatic flight (missions and recognition)

Read this before any first autonomous flight. First test the mission in the DJI simulator: this validates the whole sequence with no physical risk. For the real flight: a clear and legal location, return home armed, a finger on the emergency stop, the 120 m ceiling respected, and the drone in sight at all times.
  • On-site verification is mandatory: a mission only launches after the "Verify and launch" check, with the drone in flight, stabilised above the starting point, and the GPS reliable.
  • A mission can be refused if the subject is too far from take-off to guarantee a safe return on the battery. A refusal is a protection, not a flaw.
  • Mini 3 — one shot at a time: launch a shot, film, come back; between shots, check the battery and the space. Each shot is checked against the real terrain, but keep the drone in sight.
  • Progress is displayed ("Waypoint X in progress") and the STOP / RTH button stays accessible at any moment to take back control.

5. Special cases

Over water

3D altitude is not reliable over water. You provide the water level (or a point on the shore). The drone always keeps a margin above the surface — but stay alert, water remains the most treacherous environment.

Passages, bridges, structures

The app does not physically validate that a passage is clear. It sees the top of a passage, not its opening. You declare the width and height yourself; the recipe refuses if < 5 m or if GPS/wind are not confirmed good. Always visually confirm the passage is clear before launching.

Loyal-dog mode (autonomous GPS return)

  • To be used only in a clear, empty area, because the drone moves there on its own.
  • Rest assured: if the GPS is not reliable when it loses you, the drone never flies blind — it stays put and waits to see you again.

6. Regulations

Transport Canada sets precise rules: permitted zones, maximum altitude, distance to keep from people, visual line-of-sight requirement. Check before flying in a new place, and verify your own country's rules if you are not in Canada. Safety and legality come before the shot.

7. What the drone does to protect you

Several safeguards are built in and work silently — but they never replace your own vigilance:

SafeguardWhat it guarantees
Min stop distance 2 mThe drone never comes closer than 2 m to you on approach.
Altitude and pitch limitsA final net always caps altitude and camera tilt to safe values, even if something upstream is wrong.
Insufficient GPS → no autoAutomatic mode is blocked until the GPS is reliable enough.
No blind flyingWithout reliable GPS, the drone stays put rather than flying to an uncertain point.
Return To Home (RTH)Brings back and lands the drone at the take-off point with one press.
In one sentence: never over people, always in sight, green GPS before auto, a finger on STOP, and battery margin. Respect these five points and you avoid the vast majority of accidents.
